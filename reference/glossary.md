# Glossary

Plain definitions for the jargon this system and the hiring process use. Nothing here assumes a technical background.

---

## Hiring process

**ATS — Applicant Tracking System.** The software an employer uses to receive and sort applications. It stores your resume, lets recruiters search it by keyword, and tracks your status. It does *not* usually auto-reject you with a score — that myth is overstated. What it really does is make you findable by keyword, which is why the wording of your resume matters.

**Screen / phone screen.** The first conversation, usually 20–30 minutes with a recruiter. Confirms the basics: are you real, are you interested, are the logistics and compensation compatible. It is a filter, not a deep evaluation.

**Hiring manager.** The person the role reports to. They own the decision. Everyone else advises.

**Panel interview.** Several interviewers at once. Direct the answer to whoever asked, then make eye contact around the group.

**Loop / onsite / final round.** A block of back-to-back interviews, often several hours, in person or on video.

**Take-home.** An unpaid work sample done on your own time. Reasonable at 1–2 hours; a red flag past 4, especially unpaid and early in the process.

**Referral.** An internal employee submitting you. Consistently the highest-conversion channel there is. Worth more than a perfect resume.

**Requisition / req.** The approved, budgeted slot for a role. If a posting is up but the req isn't approved, the process will stall regardless of how well you do.

**Backfill.** Replacing someone who left. Usually faster than a newly created role.

**Boomerang.** Returning to a former employer. Underrated channel.

## Your materials

**STAR.** A structure for answering behavioral questions: **S**ituation (context), **T**ask (what you had to do), **A**ction (what you actually did), **R**esult (what changed, with a number if you have one). Prepare these once; reuse them across every interview.

**Behavioral question.** "Tell me about a time when…" — answer with STAR.

**Situational question.** "What would you do if…" — hypothetical. Answer with your approach, then ground it in something real you've done.

**Master resume.** Your complete history with everything in it, longer than you'd ever send. You never send this. You cut a tailored version from it for each application.

**Tailoring.** Rewriting emphasis, ordering, and vocabulary to match one specific posting. Tailoring changes *which* true things you say and *how* you say them. It never changes what's true.

**Keyword integration.** Using the posting's own terms where they honestly describe your work — "stakeholder management" instead of "dealing with people" if that's what you did. This is how you get found in an ATS search.

**Quantified achievement.** A bullet with a number: how much, how many, how fast, what percent. "Reduced onboarding from 3 weeks to 5 days" beats "improved onboarding." If you don't have the number, don't invent one — write what's true.

**Transferable skill.** A capability that carries between fields. A teacher's classroom management is stakeholder management. A bartender's Friday night is high-throughput operations under pressure. The skill is the same; the vocabulary is different.

## Compensation

**Base.** Fixed salary, before anything else.

**Total compensation / TC.** Base + bonus + equity + benefits, annualized. Always compare offers on TC, not base.

**Equity / RSUs / options.** An ownership stake. **RSUs** (restricted stock units) are shares that become yours on a schedule and have value if the company has value. **Options** are the right to buy shares at a set price — worth something only if the price rises above it, and often worth nothing. Both **vest** over time, usually 4 years with a 1-year **cliff** (nothing until you hit one year, then a chunk at once).

**Sign-on bonus.** One-time payment at start, often to offset something you're leaving behind. Frequently clawed back if you leave within a year — check.

**Band.** The internal salary range for a level. Most employers won't exceed a band, but will move within it.

**Total rewards.** The full package including retirement match, health premiums, time off, and learning budget. A 6% match on a $90k salary is $5,400 a year.

## Employment types

**FTE.** Full-time employee, on payroll, with benefits.

**Contract / W-2 contract.** Fixed term through an agency. Usually no or limited benefits, sometimes higher hourly.

**1099 / independent contractor.** Self-employed. You handle your own taxes and benefits — the rate needs to be substantially higher to be equivalent. *Consult a tax professional; this system does not give tax advice.*

**Contract-to-hire.** Contract with an intended conversion to FTE. Get the conversion terms in writing.

**Fractional.** Part-time senior or executive work across multiple companies.

**Returnship.** A structured internship for people re-entering after a career break.

## Signals

**Glassdoor / Indeed / Blind ratings.** Employee reviews. Individual reviews are noise; *patterns* are signal. Read the 3-star reviews — they're the most honest. Look at whether complaints repeat.

**Charity Navigator / GuideStar / 990.** Non-profit financial health and transparency. The IRS Form 990 is public and shows revenue, program spend, and executive pay.

**Tenure pattern.** How long people stay. Everyone in a department at under 18 months is a signal.

**Layoff history.** Recent layoffs mean a smaller team doing the same work, and a hiring freeze that may arrive mid-process.

**Reposting.** The same role posted repeatedly over months. Something is wrong — the comp, the manager, or the requirements.

## This system

**Persona.** Which of six archetypes you are. Set in setup, drives everything downstream. See `personas.md`.

**Rubric.** The weighted criteria that score a role 0–100. See `scoring-rubric.md`.

**Hard gate.** A dealbreaker that overrides the score entirely — missing credential, missing work authorization, comp below your floor.

**Elimination reason.** Why you passed on a role, recorded in the tracker. The patterns in these are the most useful thing the system produces.

**Cadence.** The follow-up timing rules. See `cadence.md`.

**Deep module.** An optional extra setup interview for a specific situation — career change, executive positioning, relocation, and so on.

**Key Reframing Guide.** A two-column table translating what you've done into the vocabulary a specific employer uses. The single most useful artifact for career changers.
