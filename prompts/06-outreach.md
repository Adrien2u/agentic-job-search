# 06 — Outreach

Reach a human. This is the highest-conversion activity in the whole system and the one people skip.

**Inputs:** `config.md`, `workspace/profile/profile.md`, the target company and role
**Output:** a draft message + a row in `workspace/profile/outreach-log.md`

---

## Why bother

A referred application converts several times better at the first step than a cold one. An hour of outreach beats a third hour of resume tweaking, reliably, and it is not close.

For `career-changer` it's not optional — the resume screen is the step working against you, and a referral goes around it.
For `executive` it's the primary channel — most roles at that level are never posted.

## Find the person

In order of value:

1. **Someone the user already knows** at the org, or one degree away. Fill in the "warm paths" table in the outreach log first — most searches badly under-use this.
2. **Shared context** — alumni, former employer, professional association, veteran or affinity network, conference.
3. **The hiring manager** — inferable from the posting's reporting line.
4. **Someone doing the job now.** Often the most willing to talk, since nothing is at stake for them.
5. **The recruiter** on the posting.

Cold-contacting the CEO of a 5,000-person company is not outreach. It's noise.

## Rules

1. **Ask for something specific and small.** "Would you be open to 15 minutes on what the team's actually working on?" converts. "Can you help me find a job?" does not.
2. **Lead with them.** The first sentence is about their work, their post, their program — not the user's search.
3. **Short.** Under 150 words for email; under 300 characters for a LinkedIn connection note.
4. **One ask.** Not four.
5. **Never message two people at the same org about the same role** without saying so. It gets back to them.
6. **Two attempts, then stop.** See `reference/cadence.md`.
7. **If `{{EMPLOYMENT_STATUS}}` is `employed`:** personal email, personal device, and think carefully before contacting anyone connected to the current employer.

## Templates

Each is a shape, not a script. Rewrite in the user's voice — a message that reads like a template gets treated like one.

### Cold — someone doing the job

> Subject: Quick question about {{TEAM}} at {{COMPANY}}
>
> Hi {{NAME}} — I saw {{SPECIFIC_THING_THEY_DID_OR_POSTED}}, and it caught my attention because {{GENUINE_CONNECTION}}.
>
> I'm exploring {{TARGET_ROLE}} roles, and {{COMPANY}}'s approach to {{SPECIFIC_THING}} is one of the reasons it's on my list. I'm not asking you to pass along a resume — I'd just value 15 minutes on what the work is actually like there.
>
> Either way, thanks for {{THING}}.
>
> {{FULL_NAME}}

### Cold — hiring manager, role is posted

> Subject: {{ROLE}} — {{ONE_LINE_RELEVANT_CREDENTIAL}}
>
> Hi {{NAME}} — I applied for {{ROLE}} yesterday and wanted to add a line of context that the form didn't have room for.
>
> {{ONE_SENTENCE: the single most relevant thing, with a number}}
>
> The part of the posting that stood out was {{SPECIFIC}} — {{ONE_SENTENCE_WHY_THEY_CAN_HELP_WITH_IT}}.
>
> Happy to talk whenever it's useful.
>
> {{FULL_NAME}} · {{LINKEDIN}}

### Warm intro — asking the introducer

> Hi {{NAME}} — hope you're well. Are you still in touch with {{TARGET}} at {{COMPANY}}?
>
> I'm looking at {{ROLE}} there. No pressure at all, but if you'd be comfortable making an introduction, I'd appreciate it. Here's a line you could forward:
>
> _"{{FULL_NAME}} is a {{ONE_LINE}} looking at {{ROLE}} at {{COMPANY}}. {{ONE_SENTENCE_OF_RELEVANCE}}. Worth 15 minutes?"_
>
> And if it's easier to say no, genuinely no problem.

_Always write the forwardable line. Making it a copy-paste job for the introducer roughly doubles the response rate._

### Recruiter — inbound

> Hi {{NAME}} — thanks for reaching out. {{ROLE}} is interesting, particularly {{SPECIFIC}}.
>
> Before we go further, could you share the salary range and whether the role is {{WORK_MODE}}? Want to make sure it's worth both our time.
>
> {{FULL_NAME}}

_Asking for the range immediately is normal and expected, and in many places legally required on request. It saves three conversations._

### Alumni

> Hi {{NAME}} — fellow {{SCHOOL}} {{YEAR}}. I saw you're at {{COMPANY}} doing {{WORK}}.
>
> I'm moving toward {{TARGET_ROLE}} and would value 15 minutes on how you got into {{FIELD}} — particularly {{SPECIFIC_QUESTION}}.
>
> Happy to work around your schedule.

### Follow-up — no response

> Hi {{NAME}} — following up on the note below. {{ONE_NEW_THING: an article, a development at their org, a credential you finished}}.
>
> If now's not a good time, no problem at all — I'll leave it here.
>
> {{FULL_NAME}}

_Reply in the existing thread. Add something. "Just checking in" is the message that gets ignored twice._

### After a conversation

> Hi {{NAME}} — thanks for the time today. {{SPECIFIC_THING_THEY_SAID}} stuck with me, particularly {{DETAIL}}.
>
> {{IF_THEY_OFFERED_SOMETHING: I'll follow up on X as you suggested.}}
>
> If anything comes up where I could be useful, I'm easy to reach.

_Then close the loop later — tell them how it turned out, whether or not it went well. Almost nobody does this, and it's the whole difference between contacts and a network._

## The informational conversation

If someone says yes to 15 minutes:

- Have three specific questions. Not "tell me about the company."
- Ask what surprised them about the role, what the team is actually struggling with, and what they'd want to know if they were joining today.
- **Do not ask for a job.** Ask at the end: "Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to?"
- Keep to 15 minutes unless they extend it. Ending on time is why people take the next call.
- Thank-you within 24 hours.

## Log it

Every message into `outreach-log.md` the same day: date, name, role, org, channel, type, ask, response, next action, next date. A message not logged is a message sent twice.

Weekly target: `{{WEEKLY_OUTREACH}}`. A 20–30% reply rate on well-targeted, specific messages is normal. Under 10% means the message or the targeting needs work — not more volume.
